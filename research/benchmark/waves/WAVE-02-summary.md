## Method

Independent evidence was gathered first from primary, archival, specialist and community sources. Source wording, measurement basis, variant/option/state and unresolved conflicts were preserved. SailboatData was consulted only afterward as a reference comparison; no SailboatData field value is used as HullQ evidence or as a fallback.

## Highest-value findings

### Identity / generation

- Dragonfly 32 has named-variant chronology that must not be flattened into one `first_built` value.
- Lagoon reused the `42` commercial name; the modern 2016 generation must be disambiguated from the older Lagoon 42.
- Nauticat heritage material states that the 331 received a new hull and deck in 1997 rather than being only a string rename.

### Configuration / appendages

- OVNI 370 separates ballast from a separately stated keel weight and exposes centreboard up/down state.
- Garcia Exploration 45 explicitly has twin rudders, each preceded by a protective skeg; the relationship is more specific than a single rudder-type label.
- Lagoon 42 appendage claims conflict across secondary/community sources and remain unresolved pending stronger evidence.
- CATANA Ocean Class exposes customizable geometry and daggerboard states, showing why one scalar baseline cannot represent every configuration.

### Measurement basis

- Catalina 316 explicitly labels both keel variants' displacement as `Half Load`.
- Pogo 1 uses `Light measurement trim`.
- Lagoon 42 uses `Light displacement (EEC)`.

These source bases must survive normalization because equal-looking numeric values are not automatically interchangeable derived-metric inputs.

### Reference comparison

- Some records show strong ordinary-field agreement (for example Island Packet 349 and Corsair 880).
- Other records expose definition/variant/identity issues rather than a simple numeric disagreement (Dragonfly 32, Jeanneau Sun Odyssey 410, Lagoon 42).
- CATANA Ocean Class produces material multi-field disagreement and is a priority case for deeper independent document/version research.

A reference mismatch is only a research trigger; it does not become HullQ evidence.

## Benchmark implication

Across only 17 active benchmark designs after Waves 01–02, HullQ has already encountered generation reuse, named variants, configuration-dependent geometry/mass, source-internal contradictions, cross-source appendage conflicts, multiple displacement bases, stateful boards and reference-definition mismatches. The accepted provenance/identity/configuration foundations are therefore directly exercised by real data.

This wave is research evidence, not canonical production data.
