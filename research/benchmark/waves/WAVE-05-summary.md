# HullQ Controlled Benchmark — Research Wave 05

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Designs:** 8  
**Cumulative active re-research:** 41 designs  
**Scope:** model-family / generation-boundary stress test

Wave 05 deliberately tests both failure directions in identity modelling: **under-splitting** technically different boats that reuse a model number, and **over-splitting** evolutionary Mk/suffix changes that retain major design geometry. Independent web research was completed first. SailboatData was consulted only afterward as QA/reference comparison. **Only comparison outcomes are retained; no SailboatData field values are stored in this summary.**

## B05-001 — MacGregor 26 family: 26D / 26S / 26X / 26M

**Independent sources**
- MacGregor Yacht Club of British Columbia: https://www.mycbc.ca/macgregor-buying-guide/?print=print
- Practical Sailor 26M lineage/review: https://www.practical-sailor.com/sailboat-reviews/macgregor-26m-used-boat-review/
- MacGregorSailors owner comparison: https://www.macgregorsailors.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=9265

**Observed evidence**
- The club distinguishes the D/S `26 Classic` family sharply from later 26X/26M powersailers.
- Independent sources give slightly different transition-year chronologies.
- D = daggerboard; S = swing centreboard; X = centreboard powersailer; M = daggerboard powersailer with rotating mast and twin rudders.
- Shared branding/trailerability/water-ballast concepts do not imply one BoatDesign.

**Benchmark problem:** `MacGregor 26` is a model family with materially different hull/use/board/rudder/rig semantics; suffix syntax alone cannot decide the lineage relationship.

**Reference crosscheck:** structurally useful and mostly consistent in separating D/S/X/M, while production-count applicability remains imperfect. Outcome only retained.

## B05-002 — BENETEAU First 35 family

**Independent sources**
- BENETEAU First history: https://www.beneteau.com/newsroom-actualite/1977-2022-story-firsts
- BENETEAU designer history: https://www.beneteau.com/de/designer-architecte/berret-racoupeau-yacht-design
- First 35S5 heritage: https://www.beneteau.com/first-1988-1992/first-35s5
- Later First 35 heritage: https://www.beneteau.com/first-2006-2016/first-35

**Observed evidence**
- BENETEAU's own lineage identifies an early Jean Berret First 35, a later First 35S5, and a much later Farr-designed First 35 generation.
- The later Farr generation has its own official dimensions/mass and later Carbon Edition equipment/material changes.

**Benchmark problem:** the commercial string `First 35` recurs across decades and different naval architects; Carbon Edition is more plausibly a variant inside the later generation than an unrelated hull.

**Reference crosscheck:** separate reference identities broadly validate the independent generation split, but synthetic reference suffixes are not canonical HullQ names.

## B05-003 — Moody 36 families

**Independent sources**
- Moody Owners archive: https://www.moodyowners.org/moody-boat-archive/
- Moody 36 old records: https://www.moodyowners.org/princess-records/moody-36-old/

- Moody 36S/36DS: https://www.moodyowners.org/princess-records/moody-36s-and-36ds/

**Observed evidence**
- The archive explicitly separates 1980s Moody 36, 36S, 36DS and a later 1990s Moody 36.

- The earlier 36DS is tied to the older hull family.

**Benchmark problem:** reused `Moody 36` name across materially different decades/designers, while S/DS suffixes can be derivatives of one underlying hull family.

**Reference crosscheck:** structurally agrees on an earlier/later split. No reference values retained.

## B05-004 — Hallberg-Rassy 352

**Independent sources**
- HR Club catalogue: https://hr-club.net/hr-catalogue/hr-352/
- Official rudder part: https://oldshop.hallberg-rassy.com/contents/en-us/p3718_Rudder-Hallberg-Rassy-352.html
- Official steering/skeg section: https://oldshop.hallberg-rassy.com/contents/en-us/d578_Hallberg-Rassy-352.html

**Observed evidence**
- HR Club provides designer, production, count and principal dimensions.
- The catalogue visibly contains malformed mass-unit presentation, which must be preserved/flagged rather than blindly parsed.
- Hallberg-Rassy's parts archive states all 352s use the same rudder configuration and exposes an Enderlein rudder-skeg bearing.

**Benchmark problem:** strong source authority does not guarantee syntactically valid observations; source-quality and observation-validity are distinct.

**Reference crosscheck:** strong dimensional/count/chronology agreement and a mass-unit interpretation difference. Only the outcome is retained; reference mass values are not stored.

## B05-005 — Swan 36 (1967) versus ClubSwan 36

**Independent sources**
- Nautor Swan ClubSwan 36 / heritage: https://www.nautorswan.com/yachts/models/clubswan36/
- S&S Swan Association Swan 36: https://www.classicswan.org/swan_36.php

**Observed evidence**
- Nautor's heritage identifies the original Swan 36 as an early S&S design with separated fin keel and skeg-hung rudder.
- S&S Swan Association supplies original-design principal geometry/mass/count evidence.
- Modern ClubSwan 36 is a radically different Juan Kouyoumdjian race design and explicitly exposes multiple displacement bases plus C-foil technology.
- Nautor deliberately connects the modern boat to the old model as brand heritage, not technical identity.

**Benchmark problem:** marketing heritage and technical BoatDesign lineage must remain distinct.

**Reference crosscheck:** correct structural separation but a production-count conflict exists for the original model. Only the conflict outcome is retained.

## B05-006 — Catalina 36 Mk I / Mk II

**Independent sources**
- Catalina 36/375 Association history: https://www.catalina36.org/about/history
- Mk I specs: https://www.catalina36.org/members/encyclopedias/c36-mki-specs
- Mk II specs: https://www.catalina36.org/members/encyclopedias/c36-mkii-specs
- Mk transition discussion: https://www.catalina36.org/forum/general-discussion/when-did-mk-ii-start

**Observed evidence**
- Association history defines hull-number/year transition evidence for Mk II.
- It explicitly says the underwater hull remained the same; Mk II concentrated on stern/cockpit, deck and interior changes.
- Mk I/Mk II spec pages retain the same core principal dimensions and keel-option pairs.

**Benchmark problem:** `Mk II` does not automatically mean a wholly new underwater design; generation/evolution relationship must be evidence-driven.

**Reference crosscheck:** strongly consistent with the same-hull/rig interpretation. Outcome only retained.

## B05-007 — Dehler 34 lineage

**Independent sources**
- Dehler history: https://dehler.com/de/geschichte/
- Historical Dehler 34: https://dehler.com/gb/historical-models/dehler-34-2004-2009/
- Current Dehler 34: https://dehler.com/de/sailing-yachts/dehler-34/
- Dehler Sweden history: https://www.dehlersverige.se/en/info/dehler-historia

**Observed evidence**
- Dehler's own history identifies an original 1980s Dehler 34, a separate 2004–2009 model and a current successor generation.
- The current model explicitly invokes the original cult yacht as lineage/marketing context while having its own hull dimensions.

**Benchmark problem:** builder + model number is not unique over time; multiple BoatDesign generations need explicit lineage without importing synthetic reference suffixes.

**Reference crosscheck:** reference separates multiple Dehler 34 generations and confirms the identity problem. Outcome only retained.

## B05-008 — Hunter 37 / Hunter 37 Legend

**Independent sources**
- Legend Owners model list: https://www.legendowners.com/legend-heritage/another-test-article
- Good Old Boat Hunter Legend 37: https://goodoldboat.com/hunter-legend-37/
- Sailboat Owners technical history: https://forums.sailboatowners.com/threads/non-cherubini.8654/

**Observed evidence**
- Owners material separates Hunter 37, Hunter 37 Legend, later 37.5 Legend and descendants.
- Good Old Boat distinguishes the older Cherubini 37 from later Legend-era designs.
- Long-term owners describe the old 37 and Legend 37 as radically different technical boats.

**Benchmark problem:** `Legend` is identity-critical context, not decorative text; `Hunter 37` alone is ambiguous.

**Reference crosscheck:** structural identity split agrees. No reference values retained.

# Wave 05 findings

1. Identity resolution can fail by both under-splitting and over-splitting.
2. Model number + builder is not a stable unique key.
3. Suffix semantic strength is manufacturer/context specific.
4. Marketing heritage must remain separate from technical lineage.
5. Source authority and observation validity are separate.
6. Production chronology is event-specific and may conflict among reputable sources.
7. One official page may expose multiple measurement bases.
8. Reference-specific synthetic naming must never silently become canonical.

## Cumulative benchmark position

After Waves 01–05, **41 designs** had been actively re-researched. The corpus already showed repeated evidence that a flat yacht-specification table would lose identity, applicability, configuration and measurement semantics.
