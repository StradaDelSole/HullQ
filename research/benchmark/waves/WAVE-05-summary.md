# HullQ Controlled Benchmark — Research Wave 05

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Designs:** 8  
**Cumulative active re-research:** 41 designs  
**Scope:** model-family / generation-boundary stress test

Wave 05 deliberately tests both failure directions in identity modelling: **under-splitting** technically different boats that reuse a model number, and **over-splitting** evolutionary Mk/suffix changes that retain major design geometry. Independent web research was completed first. SailboatData was consulted only afterward as a QA/reference comparison and is never HullQ evidence or a fallback source.

---

## B05-001 — MacGregor 26 family: 26D / 26S / 26X / 26M

**Independent sources**

- MacGregor Yacht Club of British Columbia buying guide: https://www.mycbc.ca/macgregor-buying-guide/?print=print
- Practical Sailor MacGregor 26M lineage/review: https://www.practical-sailor.com/sailboat-reviews/macgregor-26m-used-boat-review/
- MacGregorSailors owner/community comparison material: https://www.macgregorsailors.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=9265

**Observed evidence**

- The MYCBC guide treats the `26 Classic` as D/S forms and distinguishes it sharply from the later 26X and 26M powersailers.
- MYCBC chronology: 26D 1987–1990, 26S 1990–1995, 26X 1995–2003, 26M 2003–2012.
- Practical Sailor gives a slightly different chronology: 26D 1986–1989, 26S 1990–1995, 26X 1995–2003, 26M 2002–2013.
- D = daggerboard; S = swing centreboard. The X is a powersailer with centreboard; the M is a powersailer with daggerboard, rotating mast and twin rudders.
- All share MacGregor branding, trailerability and water-ballast concepts, but the X/M are not safely reducible to option packages on the earlier Classic hull.

**Benchmark problem**

`MacGregor 26` is not one BoatDesign with a few aliases. It is a model family with materially different board, rudder, rig, hull/use concept and chronology. The D/S relationship may be closer than the X/M relationship, so a simplistic `one suffix = one unrelated generation` rule is also insufficient. Independent reputable sources even disagree slightly on transition years.

**Reference crosscheck:** useful and mostly consistent in treating D/S/X/M separately. The reference 26D record also combines D+S production count in one note, illustrating why production counts need explicit applicability rather than silent inheritance.

## B05-002 — BENETEAU First 35 family

**Independent sources**

- BENETEAU First lineage history: https://www.beneteau.com/newsroom-actualite/1977-2022-story-firsts
- BENETEAU Berret-Racoupeau designer history: https://www.beneteau.com/de/designer-architecte/berret-racoupeau-yacht-design
- BENETEAU First 35S5 heritage: https://www.beneteau.com/first-1988-1992/first-35s5
- BENETEAU Farr First 35 heritage: https://www.beneteau.com/first-2006-2016/first-35

**Observed evidence**

- BENETEAU's own history identifies Jean Berret's early First 35 as one of the formative First designs around 1978–1980.
- The later First 35S5 is again attributed to Jean Berret, with Philippe Starck interior design, and belongs to the 1988–1992 heritage family.
- The much later First 35 in the 2006–2016 generation is a **Farr Yacht Design** hull with Nauta interior; BENETEAU lists LOA 10.85 m, beam 3.64 m and lightship/dry displacement 6,060 kg.
- The 2016 Carbon Edition adds equipment/material changes such as carbon mast and lead keel within that later Farr generation.

**Benchmark problem**

The marketing string `First 35` recurs across decades and different naval architects. A search/identity layer must connect them as commercial lineage without collapsing them into one technical BoatDesign. Conversely, Carbon Edition is likely a named variant/equipment package inside the later Farr design rather than a wholly new hull identity.

**Reference crosscheck:** the reference database separately exposes the early First 35, First 35S5 and later Farr boat (labelled `First 35-2`), broadly validating the independent generation split. The synthetic `-2` naming is reference-specific and must not become HullQ's canonical commercial name.

## B05-003 — Moody 36 families

**Independent sources**

- Moody Owners Association cloned builder archive: https://www.moodyowners.org/moody-boat-archive/
- Moody Owners Association `Moody 36 old` Princess records: https://www.moodyowners.org/princess-records/moody-36-old/
- Moody archive 1990s Moody 36: https://www.moodyowners.org/Moody_Archives/60_boat.htm
- Moody 36S/36DS records: https://www.moodyowners.org/princess-records/moody-36s-and-36ds/

**Observed evidence**

- The preserved Moody archive explicitly lists separate `Moody 36 (circa 1980's)`, `Moody 36DS`, `Moody 36S`, and `Moody 36 (circa 1990's)` entries.
- The old-family Princess records retain original drawings for rudder/skeg construction, manuals, brochures, principal characteristics and specification amendments.
- The 1990s Moody 36 is explicitly a Bill Dixon design introduced July 1996 and ceased October 2000; 118 boats are reported.
- The 1990s archive gives LOA 11.20 m, LWL 9.24 m, beam 3.73 m and three keel configurations with configuration-sensitive displacement: fin, shoal and bilge.
- The 1980 Moody 36DS archive says it was based on the earlier Moody 36 hull and gives the older family around 10.97 m LOA / 9.14 m LWL / 3.76 m beam.

**Benchmark problem**

`Moody 36` was reused for materially different decades/designers, while `36S` and `36DS` can represent derivatives tied to a particular underlying hull family. Identity resolution therefore needs explicit lineage and hull-generation evidence, not string proximity.

**Reference crosscheck:** the reference database likewise separates an earlier `Moody 36-1` (Angus Primrose-era family) from a later `Moody 36-2` (Bill Dixon), broadly agreeing with the independent split. Its numeric record is only a QA comparison; the Moody archive remains HullQ evidence.

## B05-004 — Hallberg-Rassy 352

**Independent sources**

- Hallberg-Rassy Club catalogue: https://hr-club.net/hr-catalogue/hr-352/
- Hallberg-Rassy official parts archive, rudder: https://oldshop.hallberg-rassy.com/contents/en-us/p3718_Rudder-Hallberg-Rassy-352.html
- Hallberg-Rassy official parts archive, 352 steering/skeg section: https://oldshop.hallberg-rassy.com/contents/en-us/d578_Hallberg-Rassy-352.html

**Observed evidence**

- HR Club identifies designers Christoph Rassy / Olle Enderlein, production 1978–1991 and 802 boats.
- Catalogue dimensions: hull length 10.54 m, LWL 8.70 m, beam 3.38 m and draft 1.67 m.
- The catalogue visibly labels displacement and keel-weight units as `t` while showing numeric values `6700` and `3000`; this is internally malformed unit presentation and must be preserved/flagged rather than blindly parsed.
- Hallberg-Rassy's own parts archive says **all Hallberg-Rassy 352 have the same rudder configuration** and exposes a `Rudder Skeg Bearing - No. 4 Enderlein`.

**Benchmark problem**

Even a strong source can contain an obvious unit-label defect. HullQ needs a distinction between source authority and observation validity. This case also gives unusually strong manufacturer evidence that a configuration fact is invariant across the full production run.

**Reference crosscheck:** very strong dimensional/count/chronology agreement and interprets the mass values as 6,700/3,000 kg. HullQ must not use that comparison to silently repair the malformed HR Club unit; the independent record should carry the raw defect plus corroboration status.

## B05-005 — Swan 36 (1967) versus ClubSwan 36

**Independent sources**

- Nautor Swan ClubSwan 36 page with official heritage: https://www.nautorswan.com/yachts/models/clubswan36/
- S&S Swan Association Swan 36 archive: https://www.classicswan.org/swan_36.php

**Observed evidence**

- Nautor's own heritage says the original Swan 36 established the marque, with 90 boats delivered 1967–1970 and a separated fin keel plus skeg-hung rudder.
- The S&S Swan Association identifies design #1710.51, 90 hulls, LOA 10.91 m, LWL 7.77 m, beam 2.94 m, draft 1.90 m, displacement 7,000 kg and ballast 3,600 kg.
- The modern ClubSwan 36 is a Juan Kouyoumdjian race design with LOA 10.99 m, LWL 10.64 m, beam 3.60 m, draft 2.75 m, explicit `Displacement Measurement` 2,720 kg and `Displacement Sailing` 3,390 kg, plus a C-foil system.
- Nautor deliberately invokes the 1967 Swan 36 in the modern boat's marketing heritage, but the technical designs are radically unrelated.

**Benchmark problem**

Commercial/brand heritage is not technical identity. Search should connect `Swan 36` and `ClubSwan 36` conceptually, while canonical BoatDesign identity must keep them entirely separate. The modern source also exposes two displacement bases on the same official page.

**Reference crosscheck:** the reference database separates Swan 36 and ClubSwan 36, which is directionally correct. For the original Swan 36, it reports 80 built while Nautor and the S&S association independently say 90: a clean QA conflict that should remain unresolved in reference comparison rather than contaminate HullQ evidence.

## B05-006 — Catalina 36 Mk I / Mk II

**Independent sources**

- Catalina 36/375 International Association history: https://www.catalina36.org/about/history
- C36 Mk I specifications: https://www.catalina36.org/members/encyclopedias/c36-mki-specs
- C36 Mk II specifications: https://www.catalina36.org/members/encyclopedias/c36-mkii-specs
- Association Mk transition discussion citing Gerry Douglas: https://www.catalina36.org/forum/general-discussion/when-did-mk-ii-start

**Observed evidence**

- Hull #1 was built in 1982 as a 1983 model; the last C36 was hull #2305 in November 2006 according to the association history.
- The association says the first true Mk II was hull #1368 in 1994; earlier walk-through-transom boats are often informally called Mk 1.5.
- Crucially, the association history says the hull below the waterline remained the same through the Mk evolution; the Mk II changed stern/cockpit, deck and interior.
- Mk I and Mk II spec pages give the same core LOA/LWL/beam and the same fin/wing keel draft, ballast and basic-weight pairs.

**Benchmark problem**

This is the opposite of the First 35/Moody/Hunter examples. A visible `Mk II` suffix does **not** automatically mean a wholly new underwater design. HullQ needs to represent meaningful generation/evolution changes without falsely inferring a new naval-architecture identity from every Mk label.

**Reference crosscheck:** very strong. The reference likewise separates Catalina 36 and Catalina 36 Mk II records but explicitly notes that the Mk II has the same hull and rig as the earlier version. This supports modelling the relationship explicitly rather than treating the two as unrelated designs.

## B05-007 — Dehler 34 lineage

**Independent sources**

- Dehler official history: https://dehler.com/de/geschichte/
- Dehler historical model 34 (2004–2009): https://dehler.com/gb/historical-models/dehler-34-2004-2009/
- Dehler current 34: https://dehler.com/de/sailing-yachts/dehler-34/
- Dehler Sweden production-history list: https://www.dehlersverige.se/en/info/dehler-historia

**Observed evidence**

- Dehler's own history describes the original Dehler 34 as the 1980s breakthrough performance cruiser, with the marque's history page placing the milestone in 1986.
- The Swedish Dehler history list places the earlier Dehler 34 production broadly in 1984–1991.
- Dehler separately archives a 2004–2009 `Dehler 34` historical model.
- The current Dehler 34 is explicitly marketed by Dehler as the **direct successor** to the original cult yacht, with current LOA 10.70 m, hull length 10.30 m and beam 3.60 m.
- The same commercial model number therefore spans multiple genuinely different hull generations over decades.

**Benchmark problem**

Canonical identity cannot accept a manufacturer + model-number key as unique across time. It also cannot import synthetic reference suffixes as official names. The correct model family needs multiple BoatDesign generations linked by explicit lineage.

**Reference crosscheck:** the reference database uses synthetic separation (`Dehler 34`, `Dehler 34-2`, `Dehler 34-3`) and the modern record has materially different dimensions/design attribution from the original. This confirms the generation problem but does not define HullQ's canonical naming.

## B05-008 — Hunter 37 / Hunter 37 Legend

**Independent sources**

- Legend Owners Association model list: https://www.legendowners.com/legend-heritage/another-test-article
- Good Old Boat Hunter Legend 37 history/review: https://goodoldboat.com/hunter-legend-37/
- Sailboat Owners technical community history: https://forums.sailboatowners.com/threads/non-cherubini.8654/

**Observed evidence**

- The owners association separately lists `Hunter 37` (1978), `Hunter 37 Legend` (1986), `Hunter 37.5 Legend` (1990) and later 376/380 descendants.
- Good Old Boat identifies the first 37 as the Cherubini design, followed by the 1986–1989 Hunter Legend 37, then the 37.5 and 376.
- Long-term owners explicitly describe the Cherubini 37 and Legend 37 as radically different designs; the older boat is commonly cutter-rigged while the Legend 37 is a later in-house-design-era sloop.

**Benchmark problem**

A search for `Hunter 37` can legitimately refer to multiple unrelated technical designs from the same builder. HullQ must resolve year/design context before attaching dimensions, appendage facts or derived metrics. `Legend` is identity-critical, not decorative text.

**Reference crosscheck:** the reference builder index separately lists Hunter 37 and Hunter 37 Legend, which agrees with the independent identity split. The crosscheck remains structural only; its values are not imported.

# Wave 05 findings

1. **Identity resolution has two symmetric failure modes:** collapsing different designs because names look alike, and over-splitting one underlying design because a Mk/suffix changed.
2. **Model number + builder is not a stable unique key.** First 35, Moody 36, Dehler 34 and Hunter 37 all demonstrate reuse across materially different designs.
3. **A suffix has no universal semantic weight.** `Legend` can be identity-critical; `Carbon Edition` can be a named equipment/performance variant; `Mk II` can retain the same underwater hull.
4. **Manufacturer marketing lineage must be separated from technical lineage.** Nautor explicitly connects ClubSwan 36 to the 1967 Swan 36 heritage while the technical boats are unrelated.
5. **Source authority and observation validity are separate.** HR Club is strong evidence yet contains a visibly malformed mass-unit label for the 352.
6. **Production chronology is event-specific and can conflict even across good sources.** MacGregor D/M transition years differ slightly between a marque club and Practical Sailor.
7. **One official page may expose multiple mass bases.** ClubSwan 36 explicitly distinguishes measurement displacement from sailing displacement.
8. **Reference-specific synthetic naming must never become canonical by accident.** `First 35-2` and `Dehler 34-3` are useful reference disambiguators, not necessarily manufacturer model names.

## Cumulative benchmark position

After Waves 01–05, **41 designs** have been actively re-researched. At this point the corpus is already showing repeated, independently sourced evidence that the persistence/import layer will need explicit identity lineage, evidence applicability, measurement-basis preservation, configuration scope and unresolved/conflict states rather than a flat yacht-specification table.

The benchmark remains research evidence. It does not authorize production broad ingestion, PostgreSQL persistence or autonomous web crawling.
