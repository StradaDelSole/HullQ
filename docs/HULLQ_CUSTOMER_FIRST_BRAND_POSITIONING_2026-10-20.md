## 1. Owner's two founding statements

> **„Wir geben einen Scheiß auf die Competition. Wir legen unseren Fokus ganz auf den Kunden.“**

> **„HullQ soll ganz bewusst polarisieren.“**

The Owner explicitly accepts **both product-level differentiation and a distinctive, provocative communications style**. These statements are a strategic operating position, **not** an instruction to insult customers, ignore evidence or provoke for its own sake.

## 2. First principle — Customer value, not competitor imitation

**HullQ does not follow the competition. HullQ stands for its customers.**

* We build for two customer groups: serious boat buyers and professional brokers. Neither group's convenience permits misrepresenting facts, rights, listing states or the other group's legitimate interests.
* Buyer value: less research effort, fewer false matches and less uncertainty; clear distinction between technical design facts and a concrete advertised boat; visible evidence and next actions.
* Broker value: practical inventory ownership/portability, less duplicate entry, fast legitimate changes, transparent/qualified leads, honest performance and non-coercive participation.
* We **observe** other marketplaces to learn about user expectations, minimum interoperability, operational standards, commercial practices and avoidable failures. We **do not let competitor feature lists or apparent market share automatically set the roadmap**.
* Benchmarking required by existing accepted Broker Workspace UX/Launch Gate documents **remains required as falsification and usability evidence**, not as an imperative to duplicate features or aesthetics. The Boats Group competitive register remains evidence and a considered disposition log, **not** a to-do list whose items outrank demonstrated customer jobs. Its accepted external dependency/launch constraints remain binding.
* A commodity feature can still be prioritized when it is a real prerequisite for safety, trust, accessibility, broker supply, buyer completion or the functioning marketplace loop—not simply because a competitor has it.
* Customer-first may require saying **NO** to short-term revenue, vanity growth, a high-volume but low-quality lead strategy or a technically easy feature if it makes customer outcomes worse.

**Governing shorthand:**

> **We watch the competition. We do not take our orders from it.**

## 3. Second principle — Polarize through conviction, in product AND voice

HullQ deliberately chooses an identifiable position. Being disliked by some industry participants can be a consequence of prioritizing buyer trust, broker independence and honest data. Polarization is **not a KPI** or excuse for hostility.

Product positions that can legitimately challenge entrenched practices:

1. **Truth over volume.** UNKNOWN/CONFLICT and scope-specific evidence stay explicit. No assertion of certainty merely to produce a result, score or sale. Do not confuse a design's specifications with proof about an offered physical yacht.
2. **Relevance over inventory spectacle.** More results, page views or leads do not automatically mean a better decision. Hard buyer requirements must actually be enforced, with explanations.
3. **No disguised pay-to-win truth.** Paid promotion must not secretly masquerade as an organic, technically justified match or a verified fact. Any future advertising or placement must be distinctly labeled and separately approved in product scope.
4. **Broker freedom over artificial lock-in.** Encourage authorized, rights-safe data portability, correction, transparent attribution and reversible workflows; no unnecessary exclusivity or hostage data.
5. **Lead quality over raw lead counts.** Optimize for qualified buyer interest and meaningful broker outcomes, not just maximizing submissions.
6. **Integrity over convenient commerce.** Reject fabricated status, borrowed media without rights, misleading verification and unsubstantiated market claims, even when this creates short-term friction.

**Tone discipline:** Uncompromising against broken *practices*, never dismissive toward buyers or conscientious brokers. Explain tough restrictions and make correction practical. Provocation should be memorable and substantiated; never aggressive toward an individual, condescending, discriminatory, misleading, vulgar in customer-facing execution by default, or a substitute for an excellent product.

**Distinction:**

> **HullQ can be uncompromising about bad industry practices without being arrogant toward the customer.**

## 5. Decision filter for every future relevant slice / marketing proposal

Before choosing or approving, answer:

1. **Which serious-buyer or broker job does this improve?** If their interests collide, how is the tradeoff handled transparently and fairly?
2. **What customer outcome improves?** What evidence can prove it (time saved, correct fit, known-vs-unknown clarity, buyer action, corrected listing, qualified lead, operational ease)?
3. **Would we still build it if no competitor offered it?** If not, show the concrete interoperability, trust, compliance or usability dependency.
4. **Does the proposal preserve truth, attribution, rights, privacy, security, explainability and customer choice?**
5. **Are we willing to reject short-term monetization or vanity metrics when they conflict with those commitments?**
6. **Does the communication express our position without fake differentiation, hostility, unsubstantiated claims or feature promises?**

A proposed feature is not rejected merely for being conventional; the decisive test is what it does for our customers and whether it strengthens a trustworthy marketplace.

## 6. Success metrics and incentives

Use buyer decision quality and broker productive outcomes ahead of raw impressions, search-result counts, listing volume, signups or unqualified lead counts. Valid measurable examples include verified fit/exclusion accuracy, completion and time-to-useful-search, understanding of UNKNOWN/CONFLICT, listing-correction friction, export/import fidelity, qualified leads, response and outcome follow-through. Define actual denominators and evidence before claiming improvements.

HullQ should be prepared to document cases where it declines financially attractive but customer-hostile behavior. Do not use polarization volume, outrage, insults, attention-at-any-cost or social conflict as success measures.
