## 1. Executive direction

HullQ should **not try to defeat incumbent marketplaces at sheer listing volume, installed broker reach, advertising budget or feature count**.

**Compete on buyer decision quality and trust, with practical broker value as an equally important product requirement.** A distinctive, occasionally provocative voice attracts attention; evidence-backed technical matching and productive broker workflows must earn and retain that attention.

> **HullQ should not compete for the most attention. It should compete for the most trust.**

Strategic shorthand: **Memorable positioning → demonstrable user value → measurable trust → customer acquisition**. Every claim must be earned by available and validated functionality, not the other way around.

## 2. Competitor positioning — observe, do not imitate

Established boat portals, including YachtWorld / Boats Group and Boat24, possess significant existing marketplace advantages: distribution, inventory, buyer traffic, broker relationships and mature daily-use operational tooling. These are **qualitative strategic realities**, not evidence that those companies lack search or broker features.

| Dimension | Incumbent marketplace strengths / common emphasis | Proposed HullQ acquisition position |
|---|---|---|
| Initial draw | Large inventory selection, visibility and established reach | Correct, explainable technical fit and better purchase decisions |
| Buyer experience | Broad listings plus varying discovery/search tools | Explicit hard requirements; model/configuration-vs-physical-listing truth; UNKNOWN/CONFLICT visible |
| Broker proposition | Existing exposure, leads, listing/CRM/marketing services | **Additional** source of better-matched buyer demand, authorised portability, less workflow friction and honest attribution |
| Brand | Broad trust, sales, selection and lifestyle messaging | Premium but independent; dry wit, directness, substantiated critique of broken practices |
| Differentiation | Scale, network, extensive features | Depth, data integrity and transparent reasoning for a focused sailboat buyer segment |

**Competition is a benchmark and a source of research, not an enemy or an automatic roadmap.** Keep mandatory broker usability and competitive benchmarking under the [Broker Workspace Product Direction](BROKER_WORKSPACE_PRODUCT_DIRECTION_2026-09-13.md) and the [Boats Group capability register](governance/BOATS_GROUP_COMPETITIVE_CAPABILITY_REGISTER_2026-10-16.md). Vendor feature lists, subscription tiers and marketing numbers change; refresh from date-stamped primary sources before making public comparative claims or a new investment decision. Never claim competitors *cannot* filter technically or lack broker freedom based on branding alone.

**Important limit:** More filters, prettier UI or a generic quality score are not in themselves a durable moat. HullQ's candidate advantage is technically rigorous, configuration-aware, explainable discovery **paired with truthful concrete listing evidence** and a workflow that lets brokers act on qualified interest.

## 3. Buyer acquisition — lead with a concrete problem

### Initial audience hypothesis

Focus on **serious, technically engaged sailing-yacht buyers**, especially buyers whose requirements go beyond brand, price, length and location: keel and rudder arrangements, draught, hull type, configuration, equipment, offshore intentions and other accepted structured criteria. This is an entry segment, **not** a decision to exclude ordinary buyers or to make the interface expert-only.

Do not presume that every sailing buyer wants maximal technical complexity. Product sophistication belongs in HullQ; customer-facing discovery should remain comprehensible.

### Buyer value proposition

1. Help a buyer articulate real **required vs preferred** attributes.
2. Show which **design/configuration** can satisfy the requirement.
3. Clearly separate that from **what is actually verified about this physical advertised boat**.
4. Surface **UNKNOWN/CONFLICT** instead of silently assuming a match or a non-match.
5. Make comparison, save/watch and qualified next actions available **only when actually implemented and permitted**.

### Message-to-product bridge

Approved creative examples from the [Brand Voice record](HULLQ_BRAND_VOICE_AND_WIT_2026-10-21.md):

> **Nice yacht. Wrong keel.**

> **Unknown isn't a flaw. Pretending otherwise is.**

Use them to explain an observable user problem and show the corresponding working UX. If a specific feature or evidence is missing, be transparent rather than using the line to promise perfect matching.

### Candidate acquisition channels

- **Search-engine-led technical entry pages** explaining high-intent questions and taking readers to a working, requirements-aware HullQ search. Example *topic* (not a promised live page): `Skeg-hung rudder sailboats for sale`. Any actual page requires source-rights clearance, careful evidence, original writing and reliable inventory data.
- **Community word-of-mouth and authentic engagement** in sailing groups and relevant forums, respecting rules, no spam and no deceptive promotion.
- **Credible specialist partners** (surveyors, sailing educators, offshore specialists and aligned broker contacts) when the relationship, disclosures and rights are clear.

Usefulness and genuine differentiation should drive recommendations; do not attempt volume-driven generic SEO publishing.

## 4. Broker acquisition — remove risk and increase credible value

The two-sided marketplace's most important early commercial constraint is broker supply. A buyer can try search at low cost; a broker faces setup, data-control, maintenance and uncertain demand.

Early broker conversations must answer:

- What **additional** buyers, if any, will HullQ reach, and what qualification evidence is actually measurable?
- How much work is import, reconciliation, editing and maintenance?
- Who controls the broker's records and what legitimate export or exit options exist?
- Is HullQ asking for exclusivity or to remove established channels? **No**: initial positioning should be additive, not adversarial.
- How are source, contact, lead outcome and rights handled; what costs/terms apply when offered?

**Proposed broker opening:**

> We are not asking you to leave YachtWorld or Boat24. We want to become an additional channel for buyers who know what they're looking for technically — and give you clearer context about the resulting enquiries.

This is **an aspiration to demonstrate, not a representation of existing proven lead volume**.

**Proposed broker campaign line:**

> **Keep your listings. Keep your freedom. Find better-matched buyers.**

Only deploy a variant once the relevant listing-control/portability workflows are real, accurate and operational, and the lead-quality claim is supported or clearly prospective. Never promise verified lead quality ahead of measurements.

**Tone rule:** Professional, direct, helpful and perhaps lightly witty in broker outreach. Do not lecture brokers, imply all incumbents are dishonest or suggest that adopting HullQ requires abandoning other channels. Polarisation should target artificial lock-in, fabricated evidence and weak incentives—not good brokers.

## 5. Two-sided acquisition hypothesis / growth loop

The strategic hypothesis is that a tightly integrated content/search/supply flywheel can become more capital-efficient than broad paid audience acquisition:

```text
Evidence-led, high-intent technical content
  -> prospective buyers with specific requirements
  -> explicit explainable technical Search
  -> relevant legitimate listings and qualified enquiries
  -> measurable broker value / lower follow-up friction
  -> broker participation and more accurate native inventory
  -> stronger buyer usefulness and repeat referrals
  -> [repeat only after each link is empirically observed]
```

**Not a proven flywheel.** Each link has its own failure modes: insufficient quality inventory, missing listing-specific facts, low traffic, rights restrictions, buyer reluctance to contact, broker onboarding work, poor lead follow-up or lack of repeat referral.

The network problem cannot be solved with brand voice alone. **Avoid declaring a growth loop validated from clicks, impressions or sign-ups without evidence of serious buyer decisions and broker outcomes.**

## 6. Proposed budget-constrained validation sequence

These are **suggested experiment sizes, not binding launch obligations or a commitment to immediate execution**. Selection and prioritisation follow accepted slice governance, current P0 obligations and production/pilot gates.

### A — Focused founding-broker discovery

**Suggested initial research sample:** 10–15 carefully selected sailboat-focused brokers.

Goals: understand switching/data friction, real qualification problems, current workflows, tolerance for an additive channel, test candidate messages and seek expressions of interest. **Exploratory conversations with synthetic demos are different from authorising live broker onboarding or real inventory processing.**

Record direct statements, objections, required minimum capabilities and observable signs of interest; do not count polite feedback as acquired supply.

### B — A handful of outstanding technical landing experiences

**Suggested prototype scope:** 3–5 well-researched, distinct technical entry pages with working search paths.

Choose problems with genuine user intent, evidence-backed explanations, rights-safe visuals and demonstrated working search behaviour. Do not auto-generate shallow SEO pages, make unsupportable global coverage claims, or publish if inventory is materially empty without explaining that constraint.

### C — Prove that acquisition creates better decisions

Measure:

- Buyer: relevant landing-to-search completion, time to a useful confirmed/transparent result, validated interpretation of UNKNOWN and technical requirements, quality of resulting comparison/next action.
- Broker: time to list/update/import/export, lead qualification and usable source context, response burden, meaningful follow-up and observed outcomes **when reliable data and consent exist**.
- Marketplace loop: eligible rights-safe live inventory coverage, actual buyer discovery, qualified lead conversion and observed broker willingness to continue.
- Trust: accuracy of claims, clear unknowns, customer comprehension and safe recovery from errors.

Define denominators and control/counterfactuals before claiming superiority. View or lead counts alone are not acceptable evidence of broker value.

No assumption of near-term SEO rankings, launch scale, conversion improvements or acquiring a particular number of brokers. Proof replaces predictions.

## 7. Apply deliberately polarising communications by surface

| Surface | Register | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Brand manifesto, editorial, campaign and social creative | Pointed, confidently witty, occasionally provocative | Distinctive and shareable without attacking people |
| Buyer technical landing pages | Crisp evidence, a dry line where it helps | Captures interest then demonstrates honest matching |
| Broker introduction and relationship building | Commercially serious, human, lightly witty | Earns trust and minimises onboarding risk |
| Inventory, preview, export, import, leads and technical Search | Clear, factual and actionable | Enables competent decisions rather than witty friction |
| Security, privacy, media rights, payments, errors and contracts | Precise and unambiguous | Protects customers and supports recovery/compliance |

A provocative marketing claim must never stand in for a demonstrated capability. A joke does not excuse opaque ranking, missing listing data or unsupported environmental/quality statements.

**Related accepted brand principles:**

> **We'd rather lose a transaction than lose your trust.**

> **The yacht industry doesn't need another listing website. It needs a better way of doing business.**

## 9. Questions every future acquisition proposal must answer

1. **Whose real job gets easier, and how will we observe that?**
2. **What can we prove today** about buyer technical fit and broker qualification, rather than merely hope to achieve?
3. What existing audience/inventory/rights/onboarding dependency could block the claimed flywheel?
4. Why is this message meaningfully HullQ-specific and different from generic yacht advertising?
5. Does the proposed campaign protect trust when it would cost us a click, lead, listing or transaction?
6. Are consent, source rights, clarity, accessibility, localization and all production/onboarding gates satisfied?
7. Is the test cheap enough and precise enough to falsify our hypothesis before scaling?

**Controlling acquisition principle:**

> **Use provocative clarity to be noticed; use honest technical evidence and broker usefulness to deserve the relationship.**
